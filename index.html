SAF prices are IATA average Minimum Selling Price (MSP) — the modelled production cost floor for each pathway — linearly interpolated between 5-year anchor points (2020–2030). MSP is the minimum price at which a producer breaks even; actual traded SAF prices are typically higher. Jet A-1 is live EIA spot price (US Gulf Coast kerosene-type jet fuel, converted to $/t at an assumed density of 800 kg/m³). Sources: IATA (SAF MSP), US EIA series EER_EPJK_PF4_RGC_DPG (Jet A-1).
HEFA-SAF cost stack vs. fossil jet A-1 — $/tonne, indicative 2025
Of the ~$1,200/t premium, around 55% is feedstock cost (UCO or tallow) — a volatile input linked to global vegetable oil markets. Hydroprocessing CAPEX/OPEX accounts for a further ~35%. RTFO certificate income and the effective UK ETS uplift on fossil jet (converted to USD) close ~$185/t of the gap. The residual ~$1,015/t must be recovered through airline willingness-to-pay — the central risk a project finance lender models in any SAF plant DCF.

HEFA is the dominant commercial SAF pathway today, accounting for ~90% of current production. The process is analogous to petroleum hydroprocessing and can be retrofitted into existing refineries. Feedstock availability (UCO, tallow, PFAD) is the primary constraint on scale-up — not technology readiness.

AtJ converts bio-derived alcohols to jet fuel via dehydration and oligomerisation. The pathway is ASTM-certified and well understood, but suffers from lower carbon efficiency than HEFA due to fermentation losses and multi-step conversion. Cost reduction is expected from improved catalyst selectivity and integration with existing ethanol plants.

Fischer-Tropsch is a proven technology at scale (Shell Pearl, Sasol) but those plants use fossil feedstocks. Biomass-to-liquid (BtL) FT faces high capital costs due to complex gas cleaning and the need for large scale to achieve efficiency. The technology produces a very high-quality, aromatic-free synthetic paraffinic kerosene (SPK).

PtL-SAF (e-fuel)
Up to 50% blend (ASTM D7566)
PtL (e-kerosene) offers near-zero or negative lifecycle emissions when using DAC-sourced CO₂ and renewable electricity — but at very high energy cost. The pathway is electricity-intensive (~20–25 MWh/t SAF), making it highly sensitive to electricity price. It is the only pathway not constrained by feedstock availability, making it the long-run solution as renewable electricity costs decline.
