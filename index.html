The SAF-to-jet premium narrowed from ~$1,800/t in mid-2022 to ~$1,100–1,300/t by 2025, driven by HEFA capacity expansion and growing mandate-driven demand. The 2022 spike reflects the energy price shock flowing through to fossil jet, while SAF costs (anchored by UCO feedstock) proved more stable. The UK ETS carbon price (converted to USD at 1.27) peaked at ~$104/t in mid-2022 and has since fallen to ~$44/t — adding effective upward cost pressure to fossil jet operators and partially narrowing the economics gap.
HEFA-SAF cost stack vs. fossil jet A-1 — $/tonne, indicative 2025
Of the ~$1,200/t premium, around 55% is feedstock cost (UCO or tallow) — a volatile input linked to global vegetable oil markets. Hydroprocessing CAPEX/OPEX accounts for a further ~35%. RTFO certificate income and the effective UK ETS uplift on fossil jet (converted to USD) close ~$185/t of the gap. The residual ~$1,015/t must be recovered through airline willingness-to-pay — the central risk a project finance lender models in any SAF plant DCF.

The RTFO SAF Order 2024 sets a trajectory to 22% by 2040. Mandate enforcement and buy-out price (the penalty for non-compliance) are the critical policy levers for SAF project economics — a stronger buy-out price increases offtake certainty and therefore lender comfort on revenue predictability.

Data sources & methodology
All prices in USD ($/t). UK ETS carbon price converted from GBP at a fixed rate of 1.27 USD/GBP for consistency. Jet A-1 prices: US EIA kerosene-type jet fuel spot price, converted to $/t. HEFA SAF prices: indicative, calibrated to published Argus/ICIS/IATA benchmarks and academic literature (NREL, ICF). UK ETS carbon price: ICE auction results and secondary market data, converted to USD. RTFO mandate trajectory: UK Department for Transport RTFO statistics and SAF mandate statutory instruments. Scenario projections are model-based estimates using documented assumptions; they are not forecasts.
