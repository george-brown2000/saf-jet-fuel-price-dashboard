SAF prices shown are IATA average Minimum Selling Price (MSP) — the modelled production cost floor for each pathway — linearly interpolated between 5-year anchor points (2020–2045). MSP represents the minimum price at which a producer breaks even; actual traded SAF prices are typically higher. Use the margin slider to apply a producer return assumption above MSP. Jet A-1 is a live EIA spot price (US Gulf Coast kerosene-type, converted to $/t at 800 kg/m³ density). The SAF premium is SAF (MSP + margin) minus Jet A-1. Sources: IATA (SAF MSP), US EIA (Jet A-1).
HEFA-SAF cost stack vs. fossil jet A-1 — $/tonne, indicative 2025
Of the ~$1,200/t premium, around 55% is feedstock cost (UCO or tallow) — a volatile input linked to global vegetable oil markets. Hydroprocessing CAPEX/OPEX accounts for a further ~35%. RTFO certificate income and the effective UK ETS uplift on fossil jet (converted to USD) close ~$185/t of the gap. The residual ~$1,015/t must be recovered through airline willingness-to-pay — the central risk a project finance lender models in any SAF plant DCF.

Data sources & methodology
All prices in USD ($/t). UK ETS carbon price converted from GBP at a fixed rate of 1.27 USD/GBP for consistency. Jet A-1 prices: US EIA kerosene-type jet fuel spot price (series EER_EPJK_PF4_RGC_DPG), converted to $/t using assumed density of 800 kg/m³. SAF prices: IATA Average Minimum Selling Price (MSP) by pathway (HEFA, AtJ, FT, PtL), linearly interpolated between 5-year anchor points 2020–2045. MSP represents the production cost floor; actual traded prices may be higher. Producer margin is user-configurable. UK ETS carbon price: ICE auction results and secondary market data, converted to USD. RTFO mandate trajectory: UK Department for Transport RTFO statistics and SAF mandate statutory instruments. Scenario projections are model-based estimates using documented assumptions; they are not forecasts.
