SAF prices are IATA average Minimum Selling Price (MSP) — the modelled production cost floor for each pathway — linearly interpolated between 5-year anchor points (2020–2030). MSP is the minimum price at which a producer breaks even; actual traded SAF prices are typically higher. Jet A-1 is live EIA spot price (US Gulf Coast kerosene-type jet fuel, converted to $/t at an assumed density of 800 kg/m³). Sources: IATA (SAF MSP), US EIA series EER_EPJK_PF4_RGC_DPG (Jet A-1).
All prices USD ($/t). Jet A-1 density assumption: 800 kg/m³ (mid-point of ASTM D1655 range, configurable in code). SAF MSP: IATA 5-year anchor points 2020–2030, linearly interpolated monthly. MSP is a cost floor; actual traded prices may be higher.

HEFA-SAF cost stack vs. fossil jet A-1 — $/tonne, indicative 2025
Of the ~$1,200/t premium, around 55% is feedstock cost (UCO or tallow) — a volatile input linked to global vegetable oil markets. Hydroprocessing CAPEX/OPEX accounts for a further ~35%. RTFO certificate income and the effective UK ETS uplift on fossil jet (converted to USD) close ~$185/t of the gap. The residual ~$1,015/t must be recovered through airline willingness-to-pay — the central risk a project finance lender models in any SAF plant DCF.
